Contractor, employer of record or staff augmentation: how a US company adds engineers in India

Four ways a US company without an Indian entity can add engineers in India: contractors, an employer of record, staff augmentation or a subsidiary.

By Meet Vaghasia, Founder & Lead Engineer · Published November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

In brief

  • A US company with no Indian entity has three practical ways to add engineers in India: independent contractors, an employer of record (EOR), or staff augmentation through a services company. Setting up a subsidiary is a fourth route that suits larger, long-term teams.
  • For one or two engineers, staff augmentation or an EOR is usually the simplest. Someone else is the legal employer, and the engineers can be working in your hours within weeks.
  • The main risks are a full-time contractor being treated as an employee, weak assignment of intellectual property and unclear data protection terms. All three are settled in the contract before anyone starts.

This guide is general information for planning, not legal or tax advice. Employment, tax and intellectual property rules depend on your facts, so have counsel review the contracts before you sign.

What is the simplest legal way to get two engineers in India working US hours?

Use staff augmentation or an employer of record. In both, a company in India is the legal employer and handles payroll, statutory benefits and local tax, so you are not directing self-employed individuals as if they were staff.

  • Choose staff augmentation when you want the supplier to provide and vet the engineers, cover absences and replace someone who is not working out. You direct the priorities and the supplier remains responsible for its people.
  • Choose an employer of record when you have already found the person you want and need a compliant way to employ them.

Either way, put the overlap in the contract. A typical arrangement with US Eastern time is 9:00 am to 1:00 pm Eastern, which is evening in India, so make sure the engineers have agreed to those hours.

When does a contractor start to look like an employee?

Authorities and courts look at how the relationship works in practice, not at the title on the contract. The more of these apply, the more a contractor resembles an employee:

  • They work full-time for you and have no other clients.
  • You set their hours and manage their day-to-day work as you would for staff.
  • They use your equipment, your email address and your internal tools.
  • The engagement has no end date or defined deliverable.

If a contractor is treated as an employee, the company can face claims for employment benefits and tax. A contractor arrangement is safest for a defined piece of work with a clear end. For ongoing, full-time work under your direction, use one of the routes where a company employs the person.

Who owns the code?

Do not assume you do. In general, an employer owns work an employee creates in the course of the job, while an independent contractor keeps the copyright in what they write unless it is assigned in writing.

  • With a contractor, the agreement should assign all work product to your company in writing, and state that the assignment is permanent and worldwide. Indian copyright law can apply default limits on duration and territory when an assignment is silent on them.
  • With an EOR or a services company, you need two links in the chain: the engineer assigns their work to the employer, and the employer assigns it to you. Ask to see that both are in place.
  • In every case, add confidentiality terms and keep the code in repositories your company owns from the first day.

What about tax and a permanent establishment?

A foreign company can become taxable in India if it has a fixed place of business there, or people there who regularly negotiate or sign contracts on its behalf. Engineering work delivered by an independent services company under a services agreement is the lower-risk pattern. Sales staff, country managers or an office in your name raise the question. Ask a tax adviser before anyone in India takes on a commercial role for you.

What should the contract say about data protection?

If engineers in India can reach customer or employee data, set the terms out in a data processing agreement or equivalent clauses:

  • What data they may access, and for what purpose.
  • That work happens in your environment, with least-privilege access you can revoke.
  • Confidentiality obligations that bind each individual.
  • Notice to you if there is a security incident, and how quickly.
  • Return or deletion of data when the engagement ends.
  • Whether any other company may be involved in the work.

Raise sector rules early. Health data under HIPAA, financial data and some state privacy laws add requirements that the supplier has to be able to meet, and it is better to hear “we cannot commit to that” before signing.

How should you choose?

  1. A defined project with an end date: a contractor or a scoped project with a services company.
  2. One or two engineers for months, working under your direction: staff augmentation, or an EOR if you have already chosen the person.
  3. A team of several engineers with a mix of skills: staff augmentation or a dedicated team from one supplier, so continuity and replacement are their responsibility.
  4. A permanent team that will keep growing: start with staff augmentation or an EOR, and consider a subsidiary when the team is large enough to justify the setup and compliance work.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need an Indian entity to hire engineers in India?

No. A US company can work with engineers in India through independent contractors, an employer of record or a services company that employs them. A subsidiary becomes worth considering only for a larger, permanent team.

Is an employer of record the same as staff augmentation?

No. With an employer of record, you find and manage the person and the EOR employs them and runs payroll. With staff augmentation, the services company supplies engineers from its own team, employs and supports them, and replaces them if needed, while you direct the work.

What is the biggest legal risk with contractors in India?

Misclassification. A contractor who works full-time, only for you and under your daily direction can be treated as an employee, with claims for benefits and tax. Unassigned intellectual property is the second risk, because a contractor keeps the copyright unless it is assigned in writing.
