How we work

A clear remote delivery process from idea to production.

Execution first, without burying the project in ceremony. Every engagement follows the same five steps, whether we own a scoped project or work embedded in your team. You always know what is being built, who is doing it, and what happens next.

The engagement

Five steps from first call to handover.

  1. 01

    Understand the goal

    A 30-minute call to clarify what you are building, who it is for, the systems involved and what success looks like. We tell you honestly whether we are the right fit.

  2. 02

    Scope the first milestone

    A written proposal with deliverables, acceptance criteria, assumptions, review points and the overlap hours in your timezone. Nothing starts until this is agreed.

  3. 03

    Build in reviewable increments

    Working software, pipelines or automations you can see early and often. Decisions are recorded in your tools so stakeholders stay informed between calls.

  4. 04

    Test against reality

    Reconcile outputs with source records, test failure paths and retries, check performance and access control before anything is called done.

  5. 05

    Deploy and hand over

    Go live with documentation, monitoring and a clear support or handover plan. Then we agree the next useful increment, or you run it yourself.

What you receive

Artefacts, not just activity.

  • Scope document with milestones, acceptance criteria and explicit assumptions before work begins.
  • Working demonstrations at each review point, in your collaboration window, with decisions written down afterwards.
  • Source code and infrastructure in your repositories and accounts, with credentials and access under your control.
  • Operating documentation: schedules, ownership, recovery steps, source mappings and known limitations.
  • Monitoring and alerts so a job that "succeeded" with bad data does not go unnoticed.
  • A support option sized to the system: ad-hoc, a monthly retainer or continued embedded capacity.

Working across timezones

Overlap when it matters, async when it does not.

Discovery, demos, reviews and releases happen inside an agreed window in your local time. Between those, work continues in India Standard Time and you receive written updates you can forward without translation. Response expectations, release windows and any support coverage are written into the scope rather than assumed.

Process questions

Frequently asked about how we deliver.

How long does the first milestone usually take?

Most first milestones are sized to deliver something working within a few weeks: one dependable reporting flow, one automated hand-off, one complete user journey or one bounded knowledge assistant. The exact duration is part of the written scope.

Who owns the code and infrastructure?

You do. Work is delivered into your repositories, cloud accounts and tools wherever possible, and ownership and licensing terms are stated in the engagement agreement.

How do you handle changes in scope?

New requirements are welcome; they are written down, estimated and either added to the current milestone by agreement or planned into the next one. Assumptions that turn out to be wrong are raised as soon as they are found.

What happens after the handover?

You can run the system yourself with the documentation provided, keep us on for ad-hoc support or a monthly retainer, or continue with embedded capacity. The support arrangement is agreed separately from the build.
